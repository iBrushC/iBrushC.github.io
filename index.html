About

I'm currently a sophomore at Cornell University studying Biomedical Engineering with a concentration in imaging and devices. I'm inspired by all the incredible ways biology and engineering build on each other, and want to use bioinspired design to design more effective, more cost efficient, greener solutions for healthcare and wearable technology.

My background is primarily in computer science and electrical engineering, which I still use in all of my projects. I'm particularly fascinated by simulations, low power/edge computing, graphics/rendering, and automation. I hope to apply these to the biomedical field primarily through better visualization and mobilization of advanced technologies.

Outside of academics, I'm a huge fan of rock climbing, gymnastics, photography, and all manner of arts and crafts.

Projects

I do a lot of personal, self-driven projects. Here are some of my most recent!

Fluid-based Fighting Game

I created a two to four player fighting game where you fight opponents by shooting real-time simulated fire at them. The design and gameplay were heavily inspired by Super Smash brothers, however every single attack is done by simulating a high-power jet of fire and pushing opponents with it. It's written in pure C with Raylib as the background, and runs the fluid in the background as a shader stack.

See more (Github)


Surface Micromachining

Over a several week sprint, I worked to design, build, and test a machine capable of sculpting micron-scale features into a block of wax. This was part of my goal to make my own samples of a type of dry adhesive called "gecko tape" which sticks to things with Van der Waals forces. I used an ESP32 and 1/256 substepping motor drivers to make a custom control board, then wrote a webserver-based interface to set up machining passes. I designed, 3D printed, and assembled the machine, then ran about 30 test runs. Though it could not repeatedly produce dry adhesives, it could create small ridges with ~50 microns of precision.


Assistive AI Wearable

I built a wearable watch-ring combo Bluetooth controller that uses detects customizable gestures as input. It uses a small edge machine learning algorithm in combination with statistical tests, allowing it to detect any number of gestures that can be changed at any time. It is the first device of its kind, and can reliably detect a gesture with 10 samples. This type of device could assist those with limited ranges of motion that can't use traditional input modes like keyboards and mice.

DIY Digital Camera

I designed, 3D printed, wired, and assembled a small camera in the same style as a digital point-and-shoot. It uses an electronic paper display that refreshes after each shot to simulate the process of film "developing," forcing users to slow down and creating excitement while watching the image develop after its taken.

Complex Fractal Plotting Website

I created the only website that allows for plotting of arbitrary fractals based on their respective recursive equations. It can plot every popular complex fractal (Mandelbrot, Julia, Burning Ship, etc.) and more, with support for exponentials, trigonometric, and time variant functions.

Evolution-Driven Image Drawing

I created a hybrid evolution/optimization driven algroithm to recreate images out of penstrokes. It used a custom error function to naturally allow for the evolution of artistry techniques like hatching and crosshatching. I then exported everything as GCODE and ran it on a modified 3D printer to test the algorithm in with real pen and paper.

See more (Github)

Arbitrary Black Hole Rendering

I created a rendering engine that can simulate what black holes would look like if they weren't spheres. It uses signed distance fields to create a semi-relativistic path tracer which accurately recreates gravitational lensing and the halo-like wraparound of rings surrounding black holes.
