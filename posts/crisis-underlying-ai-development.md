Title: The Upcoming AI Crossroads
Description: What happens to the industry when frontier models run on consumer devices?
Image: data_center.jpg
Tags: Opinion
Date: June 2026
Content:

While the hype of AI has remained strong, not enough people are talking about the disparity in development speeds between different aspects of the industry. In particular (as of mid-2026), the advances in model efficiency and small-model performance have been vastly outpacing those in frontier-model performance. 

<br><br>

As of writing this, Claude dropped Fable 5 yesterday, which has destroyed many previous benchmark records and been claimed by experts to be leagues ahead of all other models. The issue is that it's so expensive that nobody outside of enterprises with express need for it are able to use it. And it seems like outside of those cases, there isn't nearly as much desire to use it as well. When it comes down to it, the current SOTA is good enough for almost all competent users. I've been using Opus 4.8 for coding for a few months and am yet to run into genuine implementation errors that aren't my fault for bad prompting. <i>For many people, current models are already good enough</i>.

<br><br>

Outside of Fable/Mythos, the perceptual difference between models in recent months has been negligible. All the while, the speed of efficiency-gains has been increasing vastly. Not enough people talk about how DeepSeek V4 is 14x (<b>56x cheaper</b> with the permanent 75% discount) cheaper than Opus 4.8 while scoring roughly equivalent on benchmarks. Further, small scale models are catching up to last-year's SOTA models with a small fraction of the parameters. Gemma 12B released recently, and now outperforms GPT-OSS 120B on the MMLU Pro and GPQA Diamond benchmarks. In less than a year (August 5th, 2025 to June 3rd, 2026), <b>open models have gotten higher accuracy with one tenth of the parameters.</b> And this is roughly 80% accuracy for both, which is nothing to scoff at since domain expert humans average less than 70%.

<br><br>

Further, the spike in AI startups have resulted in insanely small and efficient models like HRM-Text, which can be trained to relatively high accuracy in less than two days and only a billion parameters. Emerging prediction tech like  multi-token-prediction have been providing further "free" gains to existing models. The efficiency increase has been exponential, being aided by every major computer manufacturer trying to include NPUs and other dedicated silicon to run models even faster. Thus raises the question:

<br><br>

<i>With all signs pointing to a close future of local models, what happens to the current AI industry?</i>

<br><br>

We're quickly nearing the point where frontier-grade models can run on laptops, and when that happens it'll be hard to justify the industry as it is. What happens to OpenAI and Anthropic when they can't charge per-token? 

<br><br>

In reality, I'm sure they'll be fine, since software subscriptions have been a thing for a while (thanks Adobe), or they might opt for per-model pricing like purchasing a new phone or laptop. The real issue is the data centers. Compute is and will likely always be useful, but up until the last few years, it has never been useful to this extent and scale. Sure, people will still be training new models, running simulations, and crunching big numbers, but the amount of compute that's been hastily thrown up in the past 3 years is staggering. It's hardly possible to believe a world where the amount of cloud compute we currently have is able to coexist with the ability to run sufficient models locally.

<br><br>

Data centers are already facing financial issues, with many being unprofitable despite heavy subsidization. Mounting public backlash is making it all the better to switch to local models, and the hardware and optimizations are catching up <i>really fast</i>. Though I don't claim to be an expert on the economics and business of the industry, it looks highly likely that the next several months to year will see a large restructuring of the industry and potentially some large players falling.
